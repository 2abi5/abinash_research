## Hard limits

| Rule | Value | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Technical content | **7 pages** | Desk reject |
| Excluded from limit | references, reproducibility checklist | — |
| **Reproducibility checklist** | **required**, in the PDF after the references | Desk reject |
| Acknowledgements | **omit during review** | Anonymity violation |
| Anonymity | double-blind | Desk reject |
| Per-author submission cap | 10 across the technical track and the AI Alignment / AI for Social Impact tracks | Submissions dropped |

Seven pages is tighter than the ML conferences. Budget accordingly: the method
section has to be efficient, and implementation detail belongs in the appendix.

## Two-stage submission — the classic loss

AAAI requires a **separate abstract submission about a week before the paper
deadline** (for AAAI-26: abstracts 2025-07-25, papers 2025-08-01). Miss the
abstract deadline and you cannot submit the paper at all, no matter how finished it
is. Put the abstract deadline in the calendar as the real deadline.

## Reproducibility checklist

Required, placed after the references, not counted against the page limit. It asks
about code, data, hyperparameters, compute, number of runs, and statistical
reporting. Answer it from the paper — if an answer needs information the paper does
not contain, add that information to the paper.

## What this venue rewards

- Breadth of AI scope: search, planning, knowledge representation, reasoning,
  multi-agent systems, and constraint work are all fully at home here, not only
  learning.
- Clean formalization. AAAI reviewers reward a crisply stated problem and a
  well-defined algorithm, with complexity analysis where it applies.
- Reproducibility, which the checklist enforces.

## What gets rejected here

- Missing abstract submission.
- 7.5 pages.
- Missing the reproducibility checklist.
- Acknowledgements left in the review version.
- A pure deep-learning benchmark paper with no algorithmic or conceptual
  contribution — that lands better at a learning venue.
