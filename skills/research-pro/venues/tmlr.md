# TMLR — Transactions on Machine Learning Research

> Verified 2026-09-30 against <https://jmlr.org/tmlr/acceptance-criteria.html>
> and the editorial policies. Re-verify per `VERIFY.md`.

## Limits

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Page limit | **none** |
| Review model | rolling submission, action editor plus reviewers, public decisions |
| Anonymity | check current policy — the model differs from the conferences |

No page limit does not mean write long. Length has to be earned; an action editor
will say so if it is not.

## Acceptance criteria — and they are genuinely different

Two questions only:

1. **Are the claims made in the submission supported by accurate and convincing
   evidence?** This is the primary criterion.
2. **Would some individuals in TMLR's audience be interested in the findings?**

What is *not* a criterion: novelty, significance, or beating the state of the art.
TMLR states this explicitly. Consequences worth understanding:

- **A negative result is publishable**, if it is well-evidenced and someone would
  want to know.
- **An incremental result is publishable**, on the same terms.
- **Overclaiming is fatal**, in a way it is not elsewhere. Any gap between claims
  and evidence gets closed on the record: you either add evidence or weaken the
  claim, *even if that reduces the apparent implications of your work*. Editors say
  so directly.
- **Clear writing is part of the criteria**, not a courtesy.

## How to write for TMLR

This is the venue where the claim/evidence ledger *is* the strategy. Go through
`../references/00-operating-rules.md`, take every claim down to the rung the
evidence actually supports, and submit that. A paper with modest, exactly-supported
claims is accepted here; the same paper with inflated claims gets a revision cycle
demanding you deflate them.

Do not pad with a novelty argument. Do not compare against the state of the art if
that is not what your work is about. State what you did, what it shows, and for
whom it matters.

## What gets rejected or sent back

- Claims beyond the evidence — the single most common reason.
- Unclear writing.
- Work of interest to no one, which is a low bar but a real one.
