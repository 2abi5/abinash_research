# IEEE TPAMI

> Verified 2026-09-30 from IEEE Computer Society author information. Page counts and
> charge figures move — **confirm with the journal before submitting** per
> `VERIFY.md`.

## Limits

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| Regular Paper | roughly **14 double-column pages**, *including* references and author biographies |
| Chargeable limit | about 12 pages; mandatory overlength charges of roughly **USD 220 per page** beyond it, assessed after final layout |
| Anonymity | single-blind |

Note the difference from the conferences: **references count**. A 60-entry
bibliography is over a page of your budget. Author biographies count too.

## What journal review expects that a conference does not

- **Depth over speed.** A thorough related work section that genuinely surveys the
  area, not the compressed 0.5 page of a conference paper.
- **Completeness of evaluation.** More datasets, more baselines, more ablations,
  and analysis of when the method fails.
- **Theoretical grounding** where the topic admits it.
- **Multiple review rounds.** Expect major revision; answer every point in a
  detailed response document. See `../references/17-rebuttal.md`.

## Extending a conference paper

The common path to TPAMI, and it has rules:

- State the delta explicitly, usually in a footnote on page 1 and in the cover
  letter: which conference paper, and exactly what is new.
- A substantial fraction of new material is expected — commonly cited as around
  30%, but **verify the current requirement with the journal**.
- New material means new results, new analysis, or new theory. Rewritten prose and
  a bigger related-work section do not count.
- Cite the conference version properly, and do not self-plagiarize text without
  attribution.

## What gets rejected here

- Conference-depth evaluation submitted to a journal.
- An extension with no clear delta over the conference version.
- Missing comparison to work published since the conference version.
- Prose that would pass at a conference on the strength of the results; journal
  reviewers read more closely and have more rounds in which to object.
